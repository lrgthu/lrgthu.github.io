*Forthcoming research note*

The question is deliberately narrow:

> **When a model predicts held-out neural responses, what exactly has been established?**

The motivating case comes from a known-truth and real-data audit of neural encoding benchmarks. A model can achieve valid held-out predictivity while the evaluator still fails to identify the generating representation uniquely. Selection over many candidates can also create apparently predictive winners that disappear on untouched confirmation data.

The note will separate five claims that are often compressed into one: predictive adequacy, specificity, candidate discrimination, generalization, and mechanism.

The empirical hook is that these claims can disagree. In controlled simulations, prediction can remain strong while model recovery is ambiguous; in real neural benchmarks, task-unoptimized and untrained feature families can remain predictively nontrivial without thereby establishing representational identity or mechanism.

The point is not that neural predictivity is uninformative. It is that **predictivity answers a narrower question than explanation**.

The standard for the finished note is to show one concrete case where a model would be accepted under a prediction-only reading but rejected under an identification-aware one.
